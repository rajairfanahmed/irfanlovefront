# Mobile-First Design

Mobile-first means defining the content and interaction constraints for the smallest supported view early, not automatically hiding desktop content or designing a cramped version of a desktop page.

## Prioritize tasks

Identify which information and actions are essential on a narrow screen. Preserve the main task and move secondary details into an understandable disclosure or separate route only when appropriate.

## Navigation

Use a clear compact navigation pattern with a named control, predictable opening and closing behavior, and visible focus. Do not hide critical account or task actions behind an ambiguous icon.

## Dense interfaces

For tables, dashboards, and complex editors, decide whether to use horizontal scrolling, column prioritization, row detail views, alternate visualizations, or a responsive workspace. Preserve the ability to complete the task.

## Touch interaction

Do not depend on hover. Give controls adequate size and separation. Keep important actions reachable without awkward precision or repeated scrolling.

## Content integrity

Avoid deleting important content just to achieve a clean screenshot. Adapt hierarchy, sequence, and presentation while preserving the user's access to required information.
