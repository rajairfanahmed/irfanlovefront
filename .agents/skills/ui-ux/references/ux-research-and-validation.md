# UX Research and Validation

## Purpose
Provide knowledge about research methods, evaluation techniques, and quality assurance for when the user requests research, critique, or validation. This knowledge should not force a research exercise into design-only mode.

---

## Research Methods

### Discovery research
- User interviews
- Contextual inquiry
- Field observation
- Diary studies
- Stakeholder interviews
- Customer support analysis
- Competitive analysis
- Market research

### Evaluative research
- Usability testing (moderated and unmoderated)
- Heuristic evaluation
- Accessibility audits
- Expert reviews
- Cognitive walkthroughs
- First-click testing
- Five-second tests
- Card sorting
- Tree testing
- A/B testing

### Quantitative methods
- Analytics and funnel analysis
- Task completion rates
- Error rates
- Time on task
- System Usability Scale (SUS)
- Net Promoter Score (NPS)
- Customer satisfaction surveys
- Conversion analysis
- Cohort and retention analysis

## Evaluation Techniques

### Heuristic evaluation
Systematic inspection using established heuristics (e.g., Nielsen's 10) to identify potential usability issues. A heuristic evaluation identifies likely problems; it does not replace user testing.

### Accessibility audit
Evaluation against WCAG criteria using automated tools, manual inspection, and assistive technology testing. Separate visual accessibility observations from verified conformance.

### UX audit
Comprehensive review of an existing interface covering usability, hierarchy, navigation, content, interaction patterns, responsive behaviour, accessibility, and visual consistency. Findings should be evidence-based, prioritized, and actionable.

### Prototype testing
Evaluating designs with representative users using low-fidelity or high-fidelity prototypes to validate assumptions and identify issues before implementation.

### Information architecture testing
Card sorting, tree testing, and navigation testing to evaluate how well the information structure matches user mental models.

## Content Design

### Content quality principles
- Clear and specific headings
- Consistent terminology
- Meaningful action labels
- Helpful form instructions
- Useful validation and error messages
- Empty-state guidance
- Content hierarchy and scannability
- Internationalization and localization
- Sample content that fits the intended product

## Ethical UX

### Ethical design requirements
- Honest pricing and product claims
- Informed consent
- Transparent permissions
- Straightforward cancellation
- User autonomy
- Clear distinctions between ads and content
- Appropriate privacy choices
- Avoidance of deceptive urgency, fake scarcity, and manipulative defaults
- Appropriate disclosure of AI-generated information
- Human oversight of consequential AI actions

A visually polished interface can still be poor UX if it misleads people, hides important consequences, or makes ordinary tasks unnecessarily difficult.

## Research Limitations

### Common limitations
- Small sample sizes
- Recruitment bias
- Lab versus real-world conditions
- Observer effects
- Task artificiality
- Cultural and demographic gaps
- Temporal limitations (snapshot versus longitudinal)
- Interpretation bias

### Responsible use of research
- Do not invent test results or user opinions
- Do not present assumptions as research findings
- State methodology, scope, and limitations
- Distinguish observed behaviour from inferred motivation
- Do not overgeneralize from limited data
- Present findings proportionate to the evidence

Reference: Nielsen Norman Group UX Research Cheat Sheet

**Foundational principle:** Research and validation provide evidence to inform design decisions, not certainty. Use appropriate methods for the question, report honestly, and maintain clear boundaries between what was observed, inferred, and assumed.
