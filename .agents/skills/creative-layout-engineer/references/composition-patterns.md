# Composition Patterns & Structural Layout Repertoire

**Scope:** Macro-Layout Repertoire, Section Archetypes & Inventive Composition  
**Owner:** `creative-layout-engineer`  
**Load Condition:** Loaded during Step 4 & 6 of Stage 2 when designing page layouts  

---

## 1. Purpose & Problem Solved
AI generators rely on a tiny subset of layout templates: a centered hero, a 3-column card grid, and a standard footer. This reference builds an expansive repertoire of structural compositions. It eliminates mandatory defaults (such as obligatory bento grids or rigid 2-line headlines) and provides concrete patterns that can be combined, adapted, or invented anew.

---

## 2. Elimination of Mandatory Layout Defaults

Previous versions of the suite enforced rigid rules that hurt originality. These are now **optional techniques**, not universal mandates:
- **No Obligatory Bento Grids:** Bento grids are an optional tool for multi-metric overviews. They must never be forced on every page.
- **No Rigid Headline Limits:** A headline should be as concise as the message requires (1 line, 2 lines, or an expressive typographic display lockup).
- **No Compulsory Double-Bezel Cards:** Choose card treatments (borderless, 1px physical hairline, recessed well, or subtle elevation) based on the approved design brief.

---

## 3. Hero & Opening Screen Compositions

Replace the default centered title + 2 buttons with purposeful spatial arrangements:

### Pattern A: The Asymmetric Pinned Split
- **Geometry:** 60% left column (or 55/45) with sticky display typography, supporting value thesis, and primary CTA. 40% right column with a kinetic vertical snap stack of interactive media.
- **Best For:** High-craft portfolios, modern software studios, luxury brands.

### Pattern B: The Docked Telemetry Masthead
- **Geometry:** Full-width top status bar with live system metrics. Central workspace viewport flanked by dual vertical navigation and property rails.
- **Best For:** Developer consoles, analytics engines, trading platforms.

### Pattern C: The Architectural Editorial Canvas
- **Geometry:** Multi-column broadsheet layout with an oversized serif masthead cutting across columns. 1px physical hairline borders, inline micro-typography callouts, and fluid staggered imagery.
- **Best For:** Publications, architecture firms, thought leadership agencies.

### Pattern D: The Full-Bleed Cinematic Focal Stage
- **Geometry:** 100svh immersive visual centerpiece with off-center typographic anchors in the lower quadrants and subtle ambient depth.
- **Best For:** Flagship consumer hardware, automotive showcases, gaming platforms.

## 4. Mid-Page Section Composition Repertoire

Vary the spatial rhythm across consecutive sections:

### 1. The Kinetic Horizontal Track
- Rather than another vertical list of cards, content is arranged along a horizontal snap rail driven by scroll delta or thumb drag.
- **Use When:** Showcasing sequential milestones, project case studies, or timeline progressions.

### 2. The Overlapping Architectural Layer
- Elements break out of rigid grid boxes: a high-resolution media frame sits partially behind an elevated text card with 1px hairline borders.
- **Use When:** Creating visual depth and editorial pacing.

### 3. The Dense Telemetry Matrix
- High-density data grid with 1px border dividers, monospace telemetry figures, status badges, and zero decorative padding.
- **Use When:** Presenting technical specifications, API endpoints, or financial metrics.

### 4. Staggered Asymmetric Masonry
- Cards and media containers feature variable aspect ratios (3:4, 16:9, 1:1) in a 2-column or 3-column alternating rhythm.
- **Use When:** Visual galleries, product catalogs, and creative portfolios.

---

## 5. Footer & Closing Section Variations

Never settle for the standard 4-column link directory:
- **The Bold Action Masthead:** Massive display CTA heading occupying the entire viewport width, with contact triggers embedded directly into the typography.
- **The Minimalist Single-Line Rail:** Ultra-clean horizontal bar with status indicator, copyright, and 3 key links.
- **The Docked Terminal Footer:** Developer-focused terminal prompt where users can type commands directly.

---

## 6. Rules for Inventing New Arrangements

When the product requirements do not fit any known pattern, invent an arrangement using these first principles:
1. **Identify the Hero Asset:** What is the single most compelling piece of evidence (code, metric, photo, model)?
2. **Anchor the Focal Point:** Give that asset primary visual weight on the canvas.
3. **Balance with Negative Space:** Use asymmetric white space to draw the user's eye naturally toward the secondary action.
4. **Test the Flow:** Verify that the eye naturally travels from Attention to Explanation to Action.
