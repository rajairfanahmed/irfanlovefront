# Accessibility and Inclusive Design

Accessibility should be integrated into planning, visual design, interaction design, content, and engineering rather than added at the end.

WCAG 2.2 organizes accessibility around four principles: **Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust (POUR)**. WCAG 2.2 defines testable success criteria at conformance levels A, AA, and AAA.

---

## Perceivable
- Text alternatives for meaningful images and icons
- Captions and transcripts
- Appropriate colour contrast
- Clear visual hierarchy
- Text resizing
- Reflow at narrow viewports
- Alternatives to colour-only indicators
- Accessible charts and diagrams
- Sensible audio and video controls

## Operable
- Keyboard accessibility
- Visible keyboard focus
- Logical focus order
- Skip links
- Adequate interaction target sizes
- Accessible menus and dialogs
- No keyboard traps
- Sufficient time for tasks
- Avoidance of seizure-inducing content
- Alternatives to complex gestures

## Understandable
- Clear language
- Consistent navigation
- Predictable interaction
- Descriptive labels
- Useful validation messages
- Error prevention and recovery
- Clear instructions
- Consistent terminology

## Robust
- Semantic HTML
- Correct accessible names, roles, and states
- Assistive technology compatibility
- Valid and reliable markup
- Appropriate ARIA use
- Reliable dynamic updates
- Accessible custom controls

---

## Inclusive Design Considerations
- Screen-reader users
- Keyboard-only users
- Low-vision users
- Users with colour-vision differences
- Deaf and hard-of-hearing users
- Users with motor disabilities
- Users with cognitive disabilities
- Neurodivergent users
- Older users
- Users with temporary or situational limitations
- Users with limited digital literacy or connectivity

---

## WCAG Implementation Areas
- WCAG conformance levels A, AA, and AAA
- Text and non-text contrast
- Keyboard accessibility and focus order
- Screen-reader semantics
- Accessible names, roles, and states
- Form labels, instructions, and error identification
- Target size and spacing
- Zoom, text resizing, and reflow
- Accessible dialogs, menus, tables, and custom widgets
- Reduced motion and animation accessibility
- Captions, transcripts, and alternative text
- Accessible charts and data visualizations
- Cognitive accessibility and predictable navigation
- Colour independence and non-visual feedback
- Assistive technology testing
- Accessibility evaluation and documented limitations

Include a strict distinction between design recommendations, testable conformance criteria, and legal requirements that depend on the jurisdiction.
