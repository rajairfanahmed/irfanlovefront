# Reference Quality, Evidence, and Knowledge Maintenance

## Purpose
Establish a reliable method for choosing, recording, evaluating, and maintaining the sources used by the UI/UX knowledge base. Distinguish established standards and evidence from opinions, trends, marketing claims, and unsupported AI-generated assertions.

---

## Source Categories

### Tier A: Formal standards and specifications
Examples: W3C accessibility standards, applicable ISO standards, documented technical specifications.
**Best use:** Requirements, definitions, conformance criteria. **Caution:** Confirm edition, applicability, and whether normative or advisory.

### Tier B: Primary platform guidance and design systems
Examples: Apple HIG, Material Design, established public-service design systems.
**Best use:** Platform conventions, component guidance, implementation details. **Caution:** May be platform-specific; do not generalize indiscriminately.

### Tier C: Peer-reviewed research and original studies
**Best use:** Human perception, cognition, usability, behaviour. **Caution:** Check methodology, sample, date, context, effect size, replication.

### Tier D: Established UX research organizations and textbooks
Examples: NNGroup, recognized UX practitioners' published work, established textbooks.
**Best use:** Heuristics, research methods, design principles. **Caution:** A heuristic is a guide, not a guarantee.

### Tier E: Professional case studies and practitioner articles
**Best use:** Practical examples, implementation experience, design reasoning. **Caution:** Distinguish results from opinion.

### Tier F: Design galleries, portfolios, and social media
**Best use:** Visual inspiration, art direction, composition exploration. **Caution:** Popularity does not prove usability, accessibility, or quality.

### Tier G: AI-generated content and unsupported summaries
**Best use:** Discovery of terms or research directions, subject to independent verification. **Caution:** Do not treat generated claims as authoritative knowledge.

---

## Evidence Record
For each important principle, record where appropriate: title/author, canonical URL, source category, publication date, last-reviewed date, applicable version, summary of supported claim, scope/context, evidence type, limitations/caveats, related entries, review status.

## Claim Classification
- **Normative requirement:** Requirement within applicable standard, law, or contract
- **Empirical finding:** Result supported by study or observed data
- **Established heuristic:** Recognized principle for identifying usability concerns
- **Platform convention:** Guidance designed for a particular platform
- **Professional recommendation:** Grounded in practice but not a universal requirement
- **Interpretation:** Reasoned conclusion from available evidence
- **Aesthetic preference:** Subjective design judgment
- **Unverified claim:** Statement lacking adequate supporting evidence

Do not convert a recommendation into an absolute requirement without justification.

## Source Conflict Resolution
1. Check whether sources address the same context
2. Verify publication and revision status
3. Prefer applicable normative requirements for conformance
4. Examine methodology when empirical claims conflict
5. Consider platform guidance within its scope
6. Document disagreement when it materially affects the design
7. Avoid claiming consensus when sources disagree

## Knowledge Maintenance
Lifecycle: Draft → Reviewed → Approved → Needs review → Superseded → Archived.
Review when: source changes, platform guidance changes, standard is revised, new evidence contradicts prior summary, limitation becomes important, repeated application reveals ambiguity.

## Source Integrity
Never invent publications, authors, version numbers, quotations, benchmark figures, or source URLs. When a reference is inaccessible, mark it accordingly. Preserve enough context that a source is not made to support a stronger claim than it actually does.

### UX principles and evaluation
- Nielsen Norman Group: https://www.nngroup.com/

- NNGroup Visual design principles: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/

These links provide entry points, not proof that every recommendation is directly stated by every source. Consult the specific source when an individual claim matters.

**Foundational principle:** The quality of the design knowledge depends not on how much information is collected, but on how accurately its claims, limits, and uses are represented.
