# Project Discovery

## Purpose

Convert a short idea into a verified understanding of the problem, the users, and the intended outcome before defining features or choosing technologies.

## Discovery dimensions

Explore only the dimensions relevant to the product. Do not send the full list as a questionnaire.

### Problem and opportunity
- What problem exists today?
- Who experiences it and in what situation?
- What do they do now, and where does that process fail?
- Why is solving it valuable now?
- What is the consequence of not solving it?

### Audience and roles
- Primary users and secondary users.
- Administrators, operators, support staff, external partners, and system actors.
- User goals, technical comfort, environment, accessibility needs, and permission boundaries.
- Distinguish a buyer, account owner, administrator, and daily user when these roles differ.

### Product outcome
- Main value proposition.
- Primary user journey from entry to successful outcome.
- Business or operational outcome.
- What success looks like for the user and for the product owner.

### Operating context
- Web, mobile web, native mobile, desktop, API-only, internal tool, or multi-surface product.
- Geography, languages, time zones, currencies, devices, network conditions, and offline needs where relevant.
- Expected user volume and usage patterns. Do not guess precise traffic numbers.
- Data sensitivity, regulatory context, retention expectations, and organizational constraints.

### Business and delivery context
- Revenue model, pricing, payment model, or internal cost center where relevant.
- Budget or cost sensitivity.
- Existing systems, data, integrations, vendors, and migration needs.
- Team capability, availability, deadline drivers, and operational ownership.

## Questioning strategy

1. Read the idea and all available project material first.
2. Identify the unknown with the highest impact on product scope or architecture.
3. Ask one focused, neutral question. Offer examples only to help the user answer.
4. Explain why the answer matters when it materially affects the product.
5. Reflect the answer as a concise proposed decision.
6. Ask for approval or correction at a meaningful checkpoint.

Avoid asking for a persona name, company size, exact traffic, revenue target, or market statistic unless the project genuinely requires it. Do not invent user quotes or personas. A persona may be role-based when evidence is limited.

## Discovery output

Record:
- Product summary and problem statement.
- Intended users and their primary jobs-to-be-done.
- Desired outcomes and success measures.
- Business/operating model where relevant.
- Constraints, assumptions, risks, and unresolved questions.

Do not advance to detailed feature design while a foundational ambiguity could reverse the product direction.
