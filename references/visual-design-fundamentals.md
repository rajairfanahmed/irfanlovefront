# Visual Design Fundamentals

## Composition as a whole experience
Design the page as one visual experience rather than a set of independent components stacked vertically. Inspect the relationship between text, imagery, open space, grid, scale, contrast, alignment, density, and movement.

- Balance, proportion, alignment, symmetry, asymmetry, focal points
- Do not make every page share the same composition. Maintain a coherent visual language while allowing each page to respond to its own content and task
- Choose layout and visual language according to the project, not as a default

## Visual hierarchy
- Scale, contrast, emphasis, grouping, reading order
- Do not put every content group inside a card. Use typography, spacing, rules, background changes, image placement, scale, overlap, alignment, and other appropriate methods to establish hierarchy. Each container must earn its place

## Grid systems
- Columns, gutters, baseline grids, alignment and responsive grids
- Recognize many legitimate strategies: centered or left-aligned, symmetry or asymmetry, single or multi-column, editorial grids, split compositions, full-bleed imagery, layered content, typographic-led, image-led, structured information-dense, sparse or expansive, scroll-led storytelling, modular systems

## Spacing
- Spacing scales, density, rhythm, negative space and section separation
- Spatial rhythm should be a deliberate, connected decision, not an afterthought

## Typography
- Font selection, pairing, type scales, line length, readability and hierarchy
- Typographic character is part of art direction, not just a readability concern
- Select typefaces for the actual brief, not as a universal default

## Colour theory
- Hue, saturation, value, temperature, contrast and colour relationships

## Colour semantics
- Brand, neutral, action, success, warning, danger and informational colours
- Do not assume that a light neutral palette, a particular font, rounded rectangles, subtle shadows, and a black primary button constitute a universal premium design

## Shape and geometry
- Form, silhouettes, corner treatments, geometry and visual consistency

## Depth
- Layering, elevation, shadows, blur, transparency and overlap
- Use surface treatments when they support hierarchy, grouping, or brand identity, not as visual filler

## Imagery
- Art direction, cropping, focal point, composition, authenticity and relevance
- Design with actual content and data whenever available
- Use existing project assets, official brand SVGs, and available data definitions before inventing replacements

## Iconography
- Metaphors, legibility, consistency, recognizability and labeling
- Where official brand icons exist in the project, use them instead of generic substitutes

## Visual rhythm
- Repetition, variation, sequence, density and pacing

## Brand expression
- Identity, personality, tone, distinctiveness and consistency
- Translate the product's real identity into visual choices rather than applying generic styling
