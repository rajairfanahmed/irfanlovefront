# No-AI-Slop Standard

## Purpose
Establish concrete rules for avoiding generic, interchangeable AI-generated design. Every visual and interaction choice must serve the specific product, content, and users rather than follow a generic formula.

---

## The Problem
A significant proportion of AI-generated designs share a recognizable template: oversized gradient text, floating blur shapes, repeated rounded cards, neon accents, glass surfaces, centered hero layouts, or decorative 3D objects. These are not categorically forbidden — their use must be intentional and suitable.

---

## Anti-Slop Checklist

### 1. No generic hero sections
Every hero section must be designed for the specific product. No interchangeable gradient headings, vague taglines, or decorative elements that could apply to any product.

### 2. No unexplained decorative elements
No floating blobs, abstract shapes, or decorative patterns unless they serve a clear purpose in the visual concept. If an element is removed and the design is unchanged, it should not have been there.

### 3. No generic card grids
Cards should reflect actual content relationships, not be used as a default layout for every collection of items.

### 4. No fabricated social proof
No made-up testimonials, fake statistics, invented company logos, unverified ratings, or generated user photos.

### 5. No placeholder copy
Content should be specific to the product. No "Lorem ipsum," "Welcome to our platform," "We're revolutionizing the way you...," or interchangeable marketing language.

### 6. No gratuitous glassmorphism or gradients
Use surface treatments when they support hierarchy, grouping, or brand identity. Not as visual filler.

### 7. No arbitrary icon collections
Icons must be relevant, consistent in style, and recognizable. No random icons chosen because they look decorative.

### 8. No decorative motion without purpose
Animation must communicate something: state changes, transitions, attention, feedback, or spatial relationships. Not arbitrary entrance effects.

### 9. No forced symmetry
Layouts should reflect content relationships, not be arranged symmetrically because it looks "clean."

### 10. No template composition
Each product deserves a composition appropriate to its content, hierarchy, and purpose. Do not repeat the same layout structure across unrelated products.

---

## What "Intentional Design" Means

Every significant visual element should have a defensible answer to:
- What specific purpose does it serve?
- Would removing it reduce the design's effectiveness?
- Is it appropriate for this specific product and audience?
- Does it contribute to the hierarchy or composition?

---

## Permitted Use of Common Patterns

Gradients, glassmorphism, cards, hero sections, animations, and popular visual treatments are all legitimate tools. The standard is not prohibition but intention. Use them when they contribute to a coherent, product-specific result. Avoid them when they are the default choice regardless of context.

**Foundational principle:** A specific product designed by a thoughtful professional should never look like it could have been any other product.
